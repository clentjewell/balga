1. SEO Objectives

The single business objective is qualified lead enquiries for garden and landscape design — phone calls, contact-form submissions and quote requests from within the Northern Rivers → Southern Gold Coast service area. Every SEO objective below is a proxy for that outcome; organic traffic that does not convert to enquiries is not the goal.

Guiding principles

  • Local intent first. A design studio serving a defined geography wins on local and "near me" queries, not on national head terms.
  • Authority compounds slowly. A new domain has no track record. Realistic timelines only — no page-one-in-30-days claims.
  • Enquiries over vanity. Report movement in enquiries and assisted conversions, not raw impressions.

2. Technical SEO Audit Framework

Balga Designs was built to the Maxxim SEO Layer 1 standard, so the technical foundation is strong. The current site already ships: LocalBusiness + WebSite + Breadcrumb + FAQ + Service/Offer + BlogPosting + VideoObject JSON-LD, an XML sitemap with an XSL view, robots.txt, llms.txt + llms-full.txt, per-page meta and Open Graph tags, a single <h1> per page, image alt text, and LCP preload on the hero. The stack (Astro static site on Cloudflare Workers) is inherently fast and Core-Web-Vitals-friendly.

The framework below is therefore "maintain + monitor" on what already exists, plus a prioritised gap list. It is ordered by impact, not presented as a flat checklist.

Priority 1 — Highest impact (do first / at go-live)

  • Indexability flip. The single most important technical action: confirm the noindex is removed at go-live on the production domain and the workers.dev preview stays noindex. Verify there is no stray Disallow: / or preview-only header in production robots.txt / meta.
  • Canonical hygiene. Every production URL must self-canonical to the balgadesigns.com.au apex (or chosen www/non-www) — never to the preview host. One canonical host, enforced by 301 redirect for the other variant, plus HTTPS-only.
  • Search Console + sitemap submission. Verify the production property, submit sitemap.xml, and watch the Coverage/Indexing report for exclusions.
  • Content gaps that are also technical gaps — per-service and per-suburb pages. These are the highest-leverage build items: dedicated indexable pages for each core service (Landscape Design, Garden Facelift, Decorative Plants/pot styling, Integrated Pest Management, Weed Control Consultation, Horticulture Consultation) and for each core suburb (Lennox Head, Ballina, Byron Bay). Without them there is nothing for suburb/service queries to rank. Each needs its own URL, title, H1, unique body and Service schema.

Priority 2 — Local trust signals (high impact for this business type)

  • Google Business Profile — not on-site, but the dominant local ranking asset. Claim, verify, complete (see Section 6).
  • Reviews — establish the review-generation flow (Section 6). Also a ranking + conversion signal.
  • NAP consistency — one source of truth for Name/Address/Phone across LocalBusiness JSON-LD, footer, contact page and GBP. As a service-area business, suppress the street address publicly where appropriate and define the service area explicitly.

Priority 3 — Maintain & monitor (already shipping; keep healthy)

  • Structured data: keep the LocalBusiness/Service/FAQ/BlogPosting JSON-LD valid as content changes; re-validate in the Rich Results Test after template changes. Add Service schema to the new per-service pages.
  • Core Web Vitals: Astro-on-Workers should hold LCP/INP/CLS in "Good". Monitor field data in the Search Console CWV report once traffic exists; guard against regressions from added images, embeds or the video (VideoObject) hero. Keep the LCP preload aligned with the actual largest element.
  • Mobile: confirm mobile-first rendering, tap targets and readable fonts on the real device profile of local users.
  • Site structure & URLs: shallow, readable, keyword-aware paths (see Section 5 URL conventions); logical hub → cluster hierarchy.
  • Redirects: keep a clean 301 map; avoid chains; watch for soft-404s on thin pages.
  • sitemap.xml / robots.txt / llms.txt: keep in sync as pages are added — new service/suburp pages must appear in the sitemap.

How to prioritise ongoing

Rank each finding by (impact on local enquiries) × (number of pages affected) ÷ (effort). Indexability and per-suburb/service pages beat micro-optimisations every time.

4. Content Strategy for SEO

Pillar / cluster model

Adopt a hub-and-spoke topic model so authority concentrates rather than scatters.

  • Hub (pillar): a comprehensive "Native & Sustainable Garden Design" guide covering what native/sustainable design is, why it matters in the Northern Rivers climate, the design process, and low-maintenance/water-wise principles. It links out to every cluster and every cluster links back to it.
  • Clusters (spokes):
    • Blog/informational spokes: the 3 existing articles (What are Native Gardens?; Why Designing & Planning Matter; Balga — Meaning & Purpose) plus new pieces on low-maintenance/water-wise natives and native plants for the region.
    • Service spokes: the six service pages from Section 3, each linking up to the pillar and across to relevant articles.
    • Local spokes: the suburb/area pages, which anchor the pillar to geography.

Build on what exists

The three published articles are the seed of the cluster. Actions: interlink them with each other and the (new) pillar; refresh each with a clear FAQ block and a call-to-action toward the relevant service; ensure each targets a distinct Cluster F / informational keyword so they do not overlap.

Gap-analysis approach

Rather than guessing topics: (1) once indexed, mine Search Console for queries the site already surfaces for but ranks 8–20 — these are quick-win content gaps; (2) compare the keyword map (Section 3) against existing URLs to find clusters with no page (currently: all service pages, all suburb pages, the pillar, water-wise and regional-plants articles); (3) pull "People Also Ask" and autocomplete for the core terms to source real question phrasing for FAQ content; (4) review the top 3 competitors (Section 8) for themes present on their sites and absent here.

Publishing cadence (realistic)

  • Months 0–3: prioritise the structural pages (service + suburb + pillar) over new blog posts — these unlock rankings fastest. Aim to ship the service and suburb pages in this window.
  • Months 3–12: one well-researched article or one refreshed existing page per 3–4 weeks. Consistency beats volume; a lone studio should not over-commit and then stall.

Brief requirements per cluster piece

  • Target primary keyword + 2–4 secondary/PAA variants (from real query data, not invented).
  • Search intent and the reader's next step (which service/CTA it feeds).
  • Recommended word count anchored to what currently ranks for the term — not an arbitrary number.
  • Required H2/H3 subtopics; at least one FAQ block phrased as people actually ask (supports GEO — see Section 9).
  • Internal links: up to the pillar, across to 1–2 sibling pieces, out to the relevant service/suburb page.
  • Original imagery of Balga's own work with descriptive, plant/location-aware alt text.
  • Brand voice: regenerative, native, sustainable, "rooted in Country", naturalistic — never generic landscaping copy.

5. On-Page Optimisation

Title & meta description formulas (with examples)

  • Title formula: Primary Keyword | Location | Balga Designs — keep under ~60 characters.
    • Home: Native & Sustainable Garden Design | Northern Rivers NSW | Balga Designs
    • Suburb: Garden Design Lennox Head | Native & Sustainable | Balga Designs
    • Service: Garden Facelift & Refresh | Northern Rivers | Balga Designs
  • Meta description formula: value + place + differentiator + soft CTA, ~150 characters.
    • Example: "Naturalistic native and sustainable garden design across the Northern Rivers, from Lennox Head to Byron Bay. Rooted in Country. Get a quote from Balga Designs."

Heading guidance (H1–H3)

  • One H1 per page containing the primary keyword naturally (e.g. "Native Garden Design in Ballina").
  • H2s for major sections and the secondary intents (process, plants, service area, FAQ).
  • H3s for sub-points and individual FAQ questions. Never skip levels; headings describe content, they are not decoration.

Internal linking

  • Hub-and-spoke: every service, suburb and article links back to the pillar; the pillar links out to all.
  • Contextual, descriptive anchor text ("low-maintenance native gardens"), never "click here".
  • Each service page links to the suburbs it serves and vice-versa; each article links to the service it supports.
  • Keep important pages within 2–3 clicks of the home page.

Image optimisation

  • Use Balga's own project photography; compress and serve modern formats (Astro can output WebP/AVIF).
  • Descriptive alt text with plant/location context where genuine ("coastal native garden with grass trees, Lennox Head").
  • Explicit width/height (or aspect-ratio) to protect CLS; keep the hero as the preloaded LCP element.
  • Meaningful file names (native-garden-lennox-head.jpg), not IMG_1234.jpg.

URL conventions

  • Lowercase, hyphenated, keyword-bearing, shallow: /services/garden-facelift/, /garden-design-ballina/, /blog/what-are-native-gardens/.
  • No dates, IDs or stop-word clutter; stable slugs (avoid renaming — if you must, 301 the old URL).
  • Trailing-slash convention chosen once and enforced site-wide via canonical + redirect.

For a service-area business chasing local enquiries, local SEO is the highest-ROI channel — often ahead of classic organic. Balga operates from Lennox Head and serves the Northern Rivers through to the Southern Gold Coast; the map pack and GBP are where most local buyers make first contact.

Google Business Profile optimisation checklist

  • Claim & verify the profile (existing GBP).
  • Configure as a service-area business: hide the street address, define the service area by suburb/region (Lennox Head, Ballina, Byron Bay, Northern Rivers → Southern Gold Coast).
  • Categories: primary "Landscape designer"; secondary as relevant (e.g. "Garden designer", "Landscaper", "Horticulturist").
  • Exact NAP: Balga Designs / 041 373 1670 / info@balgadesigns.com.au — identical to the site's LocalBusiness JSON-LD and footer.
  • Hours: Mon–Fri 07:00–16:00 AEST.
  • Services & pricing: list all six services; where comfortable, publish the real package prices (Small NativeScapes $2,500; Potted Greenery $2,000; Balga Inspo $2,000; Complete Design $4,500).
  • Description & attributes: keyword-aware, brand-true ("sustainable native garden design rooted in Country"); set attributes (e.g. LGBTQ-friendly, online estimates) if accurate.
  • Photos: regular, geo-relevant project photos; add the intro/hero video.
  • Google Posts: post updates/offers/new projects periodically for freshness.
  • Products/Q&A: seed the GBP Q&A with the same FAQs used on-site.
  • Website link: point to the production domain (not the preview) once live; ideally to the most relevant local landing page.

Citations

Build consistent NAP citations to reinforce local relevance. Cross-reference the Maxxim citations playbook (maxxim-citations-playbook) to generate the client-specific, prioritised, ready-to-paste submission list using Balga's exact NAP. Priority order for this business:

  1. Google Business Profile (done above — the anchor citation).
  2. Core aggregators/directories: Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, True Local, Yellow Pages AU, Yelp AU, Hotfrog, Startlocal.
  3. Industry & niche: Houzz (see Section 7), AILDM and Landscaping NSW member/find-a-pro listings, local nursery/native-plant supplier "find a designer" pages.
  4. Local/regional: Northern Rivers business directories, Ballina/Byron/Lennox chamber-of-commerce and community listings, tourism/regional guides.

Keep NAP byte-for-byte identical everywhere; inconsistent citations dilute the signal. Track submissions in the playbook's checklist.

Review-generation strategy

  • Make Google reviews a standard step at project sign-off; send a direct GBP review link (short link) by SMS/email while satisfaction is high.
  • Aim for a steady drip (a few per month) rather than a spike — velocity and recency matter more than a one-off batch.
  • Respond to every review, positive or negative, in brand voice — a ranking and trust signal.
  • Encourage reviewers to mention the suburb and service naturally ("native garden design in Ballina") — never scripted or incentivised.
  • Seed a first cohort from recent happy clients at go-live.

Locally-targeted content

  • The suburb/area pages (Section 3) are the on-site backbone of local SEO — genuinely localised, not templated: local climate, coastal-native palettes, regional projects, service-area statement.
  • Embed a service-area map and reference nearby landmarks/regions honestly.
  • Blog occasionally on regional themes ("native plants for Northern Rivers coastal gardens") to reinforce geographic relevance and earn local links.

9. AI Search & SGE / GEO Considerations

How AI overviews & zero-click affect these queries

Google AI Overviews and AI assistants increasingly answer informational queries directly, meaning some "what are native gardens" / "low-maintenance native garden" traffic never clicks through (zero-click). However, local, transactional intent is more resilient — "garden designer near me / in Ballina", quotes, and contact actions still route to the map pack, GBP and the site. The strategic response: don't chase pure-informational clicks that AI absorbs; instead earn citations in AI answers (brand exposure) while owning the local commercial queries that still convert.

Formats that earn AI citations (Balga already ships most)

  • Concise, quotable answers: lead each page/section with a direct 1–2 sentence answer to the implied question, then elaborate — the pattern LLMs lift as citations.
  • FAQ blocks phrased the way people actually ask Google/AI (use the maxxim-seo-content skill to draft these), backed by FAQ JSON-LD (already shipping).
  • Structured data: the existing LocalBusiness/Service/FAQ/BlogPosting JSON-LD helps machines parse entity, services and location — maintain and extend to new pages.
  • llms.txt + llms-full.txt: already shipped — keep them current so AI crawlers get a clean, accurate summary of who Balga is, where it serves and what it offers.
  • Clear entity signals & consistent NAP across site, schema and GBP so AI systems resolve Balga as a real, located business.

Opportunities

  • Being an early, well-structured, GEO-ready local site in a niche (native/sustainable design, Northern Rivers) is a genuine edge for AI citation.
  • Region-specific linkable assets (native-plant guides) are exactly what AI answers cite for "best native plants for coastal NSW gardens".

Mitigations

  • Assume some informational impressions won't convert to clicks; weight content toward commercial/local intent and brand-building.
  • Track AI-overview presence qualitatively (spot-check key queries) since precise attribution is limited.
  • Keep facts, prices and service areas accurate and current so AI representations of Balga stay correct.

10. Measurement & Reporting

Golden rule: report movement and trend, never guaranteed positions. Rankings fluctuate; the story is direction over time against the baseline captured at go-live.

Tools

  • Google Search Console — impressions, clicks, average position, CTR, query/page data, indexing, Core Web Vitals field data, links. The primary source of truth for organic.
  • GA4 or a privacy-first analytics option (e.g. Cloudflare Web Analytics / Plausible) — sessions, engaged sessions, source/medium, conversions.
  • Rank tracking — a tool tracking the target suburb/service keywords for the local geography (local-pack + organic).
  • GBP Insights / Performance — searches, views, calls, direction requests, website clicks, review count/rating.
  • Call/enquiry tracking — form-submission events and (optionally) call tracking to attribute enquiries to organic.

All checkpoint outcomes are targets to steer by, reported as movement against baseline — never as guaranteed rankings.
