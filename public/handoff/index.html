Your new website — what changed, and what happens next

The written handover for the rebuild of balgadesigns.com.au: what we changed and why it matters, the search and AI-search work that shipped with it, every update you asked for and where it landed, and the last few steps to go live.

Everything on the Updates requested tab comes straight from our email thread “Balga Designs, your website upgrades are done (and it’s flying)” (29 July – 20 August 2026). Each item quotes what you asked for, on the day you asked, and says exactly where it ended up.

What changed, in plain English

Your website looks like your website. Underneath, almost nothing is the same. The old WordPress and page-builder engine is gone, replaced by lightweight hand-coded pages served from Cloudflare’s global network. That single change is where most of the speed, the security and the lower running cost come from.

Jumping from the 60s into the 90s matters beyond bragging rights: Google uses speed as a ranking factor, a fast site keeps visitors reading instead of leaving, and more of them turn into enquiries.

1. Your site moved to Cloudflare

The site is served from Cloudflare’s edge network, so pages load from a location near whoever is visiting. You also get their security layer in front of the site by default — no plugin to buy, keep updated or renew.

2. Rebuilt as clean, modern code

We rebuilt Balga Designs from the ground up as hand-written pages. No WordPress, no Elementor, no plugin stack, no theme updates to chase. Nothing on the page is loaded that the page doesn’t actually need, which is why it is so much quicker and so much cheaper to maintain.

3. You can edit it yourself

The site comes with its own Content Manager — a private editor at /admin/ with your own login. From there you can change FAQs, services, projects, pricing, images, page text and the shared details (phone, email, hours, service area) that appear across the site.

  • A media library so photos are uploaded once and reused anywhere.
  • Publish / unpublish per page, so a page you’re not ready to show can be taken down without deleting it.
  • An enquiry inbox holding every message the contact form receives.
  • A plain-language change feed, so you can see what was edited and when.

Every save is recorded and the site rebuilds and republishes itself automatically — there’s no separate “publish to the server” step for you to remember.

4. The contact form actually delivers

Enquiries are checked on the server before they’re accepted, saved to your inbox in the editor, and emailed to you. If email delivery isn’t configured, the form says so honestly rather than pretending a message was sent — so a lost enquiry can’t hide.

5. Security, quietly, in the background

Nothing you need to manage, but worth knowing it’s there: strict browser security headers on every response, a content policy that blocks injected scripts, sign-in rate limiting on the editor, hardened login sessions, and the admin area kept out of search results entirely.

Nothing about the way you work changes. Same brand, same photos, same words — just a much faster, safer, easier-to-edit version of the site you already had.

SEO & GEO — built into the pages

Two audiences have to understand your website. SEO is the familiar one: helping Google rank you when someone searches “native garden designer Ballina”. GEO — Generative Engine Optimisation — is the newer one: helping the AI assistants people now ask instead (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Google’s AI answers) describe your business correctly when they answer on your behalf.

Both come down to the same thing: state plainly, in a form machines can read, who you are, what you do, where you do it and what it costs. That is what the rebuild does.

What Google sees

  • A business record on every page — structured data describing Balga Designs as a local business: name, phone, email, address, hours, service area, map position, social profiles and the full list of services with prices.
  • Service, pricing and FAQ markup — your six services and four packages are described as real offers, and the FAQ page is marked up so its questions can appear directly in search results.
  • A page per service, rather than one page listing everything, so each service can rank for its own searches.
  • Breadcrumbs, article and video markup so guides and the homepage video are understood as what they are.
  • A sitemap at /sitemap.xml (with a readable view for humans) and a robots.txt that welcomes search crawlers.
  • Written-once page details — a unique title, description, canonical address and branded share image on every page, so links posted to Facebook, Instagram or a text message preview properly.
  • Clean page structure — one main heading per page, headings in order, alt text on every image, and image sizes declared up front so nothing jumps around as the page loads.

What AI assistants see

  • llms.txt — a plain-English summary of the business, its services and its contact details, published in the format AI answer engines look for.
  • llms-full.txt — the full readable text of the site in one file, so an assistant quoting you quotes your words rather than guessing.
  • Named permission for AI crawlers — GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended and others are explicitly allowed, so your site is eligible to be used as a source rather than silently skipped.
  • Answer-first writing — each FAQ opens with a short, direct, quotable sentence, then explains. That first sentence is what an assistant repeats.

Local signals

Lennox Head, NSW is stated once in a single source and flows to every place it appears — the business record, the page footers, the location tags, the sitemap and llms.txt. It can’t drift out of step, which is exactly what Google looks for when deciding whether two mentions of a business are the same business. Service area is stated the same way: the Northern Rivers through to the Southern Gold Coast, including Lennox Head, Ballina and Byron Bay.

Checked on every build

All of the above is verified automatically each time the site is rebuilt. If a page ever loses its description, its business record, an image’s alt text or its share image, the build reports it rather than quietly publishing a weaker page.

What’s still yours to do (off the website)

On-page work is done. The rest of local search happens off your site, and it’s where the next real gains are:

  • Google Business Profile — the single biggest lever for “near me” searches. Claim it, fill in every field, and match the name, address and phone exactly as they appear on the site.
  • Directory listings — we’ve prepared a prioritised list of 26 places to list Balga Designs, with the exact wording to paste in, so every listing agrees with every other one.
  • Reviews — steady, genuine Google reviews from happy clients move local rankings more than almost anything else you can do.

Your socials person can work through this, or we can quote it as a small package — just say the word.

Going live, and what happens after

The site is approved and built. What’s left is pointing your domain at it and checking everything answers correctly on the real address.

The nameserver change is the actual switch: your domain stops pointing at the old host and starts pointing at Cloudflare. Domain changes take a little while to spread around the world, so for a few hours some people may still be served the old site. Nothing is lost in that window — both versions answer.

The moment it’s live

  • The site becomes visible to search engines. Until then it is deliberately hidden, so the preview can’t compete with your real site in Google.
  • The sitemap is submitted to Google Search Console so the new pages are found quickly rather than waiting to be stumbled upon.
  • Contact-form email starts delivering from your own domain.
  • Your editor login keeps working exactly as it does now — same address, same password, just on the live site.

Then, in order of value

  1. Google Business Profile — claimed, complete, and matching the site word for word.
  2. Directory listings — work through the prepared list of 26, using the same wording each time.
  3. Reviews — ask happy clients. This is the cheapest local-search win there is.
  4. Photographs — new project photos as you finish gardens; the site is built to take them without any developer involvement.
  5. Gallery page — once you’ve sent a brief and we’ve quoted it.

Looking after it

There is no plugin stack to update, no theme to renew and no security patching cycle to keep on top of. The site is static files served from Cloudflare, which is why it is both fast and quiet. Your day-to-day is simply: edit in the Content Manager, save, and the site updates itself.
